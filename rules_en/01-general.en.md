## Introduction  
<span id="GEN-1"></span>  

**GEN-1.1**  
The aim of the event is to evaluate fencing in a safe and friendly manner, according to the principle “hit without being hit”. Similarly to historical rulesets, the competition tests deliberately abstracted skills; it is not a simulation of an unrestricted fight continued to the bitter end. In this sense, every point is treated as a separate “duel” within the framework of the rules below; losing such a duel is indicated by a point awarded against the losing fencer. Our goal is to establish a relative ranking and identify, over the competition day(s), the fencer who proves best in tactical, technical and athletic ability.  

**GEN-1.2**  
A fundamental principle above all others is sportsmanship. Any workable ruleset must be effective in ensuring equal opportunity for all participants.

## Explanation of the technical terms most commonly used in refereeing  
<span id="GEN-3"></span>  

Explanation of the technical terms most commonly used in judging fencing actions.  

### Preamble  
It is emphasised that this section does not replace fencing treatises; it is included to help the reader understand the rules.  

### Fencing time  
<span id="GEN-3.1"></span>  

Fencing time is the time required to perform one simple fencing action.  

### Offensive and defensive actions  
<span id="GEN-3.2"></span>  

#### Definitions  

**GEN-3.2.1**  
Offensive actions (attack, riposte, counter-riposte).  

**GEN-3.2.1.1**  
**Attack** — the initial offensive action, made by extending the arm and continuously threatening the opponent’s valid target, preceding the initiation of the lunge, flèche, or other weapon-specific offensive footwork. 

**GEN-3.2.1.2**  
**Riposte** — the offensive action made by the fencer who has parried the attack.  

**GEN-3.2.1.3**  
**Counter-riposte** — the offensive action made by the fencer who has parried the riposte.  

**GEN-3.2.2**  
Defensive actions are called parries.  

- A parry is a defensive action made with the weapon to prevent an offensive action from arriving.  

#### Explanations  

##### Offensive actions  

**GEN-3.2.3**  
**Attack**  

**GEN-3.2.3.1**  
An action is **simple** when it is executed in one movement:
- either **direct** (in the same line),
- or **indirect** (in another line).  

**GEN-3.2.3.2**  
An action is **compound** when it is executed in several movements.  

**GEN-3.2.4**  
**Riposte** (thrust/cut back)  
A riposte may be **immediate** or **delayed**, depending on the nature and speed of the action. The riposte may be:  

**GEN-3.2.4.1**  
- **Simple, direct**
- **Direct riposte**: hits without leaving the line in which the parry was made.
- **Riposte along the blade**: hits by grazing along the blade after the parry.  

**GEN-3.2.4.2**  
**Simple, indirect**
- **Riposte by disengagement**: hits in the opposite line to that in which the parry was formed.
- **Riposte with a coupé**: hits in the opposite line to that in which the parry was formed, the blade passing over the opponent’s point.  

**GEN-3.2.4.3**  
**Compound riposte**
- **Riposte with a doublé**: hits in the opposite line after describing a full circle around the opponent’s blade.
- **Riposte with a one–two**: hits in the same line after first having gone into the opposite line by passing under the opponent’s blade.  

**GEN-3.2.5**  
Counter-attacks  

Counter-attacks are offensive or offensive–defensive actions made during the opponent’s offensive action.  

**GEN-3.2.5.1**  
**Stop hit**: a counter-attack made into an attack.  

**GEN-3.2.5.2**  
**Stop hit with opposition**: a stop hit made while closing the line in which the opponent’s attack will be completed.  

**GEN-3.2.5.3**  
**Stop hit made within a period of fencing time (“in time”)**: a stop hit made at the moment of the attacker’s hesitation, feint, or badly executed attack. In Longsword-VOR, a started attack has priority; a stop hit is considered successful only if clearly “in tempo” (it lands before the final movement of the attack begins) or the attacker does not hit at all.  

**GEN-3.2.6**  
Other offensive actions — a further offensive action after the initial attack.  

**GEN-3.2.6.1**  
**Remise**: a simple immediate offensive action, following the original attack without withdrawing the arm, after the opponent has parried or retreated, when the opponent either breaks blade contact without riposting or makes a delayed/indirect riposte, or makes a compound riposte.  

**GEN-3.2.6.2**  
**Redoublement**: a new simple or compound action against an opponent who has parried without riposting, or who avoided the first action merely by retreating or displacing the target.  

**GEN-3.2.6.3**  
**Reprise of the attack**: a new attack executed immediately after a return to the on-guard position.  

**GEN-3.2.6.4**  
**Counter-time**: any action made by the attacker against a stop hit made by the opponent.  

##### Defensive actions  

**GEN-3.2.7**  
Parry  
- **A parry is simple, direct** when it is made in the same line as the attack.  
- **A circular parry** is made in the opposite line to that of the attack.  

#### “Point in line” position  

**GEN-3.2.8**  
The point in line position is a specific position in which the fencer’s arm is kept extended and the point of the weapon continually threatens the opponent’s valid target.  

## The field of play  
<span id="GEN-4"></span>  
<!-- TODO -->  

**GEN-4.1**  
The surface of the field of play is even and horizontal. It must not provide advantage or disadvantage to either fencer, especially with respect to lighting.  

**GEN-4.2**  
That portion of the field of play which is used for fencing is called the piste.  

**GEN-4.3**  
The piste/arena is square or rectangular. The minimum permitted bounding size is 6×9 metres and the maximum is 8×12 metres. The boundaries must be marked along their full length in a way that adheres well to the floor. The nearest obstacle must be at least 1 metre from the boundary.  

**GEN-4.4**  
The starting positions of the fencers must be clearly marked on the floor such that they are at least 2 metres from the centre, leaving at least 4 metres between the two fencers at the start of the bout.  

## Equipment  
<span id="GEN-5"></span>  

### Compliance  
<span id="GEN-5.1"></span>  

**GEN-5.1.1**  
Fencers arm, equip and clothe themselves and fence at their own responsibility and at their own risk.  

**GEN-5.1.2**  
At the venue of official MHS competitions (including training halls linked to the competition), any fencer who warms up or trains must wear fencing clothing and equipment complying with MHS requirements. Any person giving a lesson must wear at least an MHS-compliant master’s plastron, glove and mask. Any fencer taking a lesson must wear at least a mask and a glove. The MHS Supervisor of the competition or a member of the Organising Team penalises any person not respecting this rule with a yellow card, followed by a black card in case of repeated infringement.  

**GEN-5.1.3**  
The safety measures specified in this ruleset and the attached standards are designed to reinforce safety but cannot guarantee it. Responsibility cannot be transferred to MHS, organisers, officials, or any person who may cause an accident.  

### For steel weapons  
<span id="GEN-5.2"></span>  

**GEN-5.2.1**  
#### Head protection  

**GEN-5.2.1.1**  
An undamaged FIE-approved mask (CE level 2, 1600N resistance) is mandatory.  

**GEN-5.2.1.2**  
A HEMA-specific back-of-head and cervical spine protector compatible with the mask is mandatory.  

**GEN-5.2.1.3**  
A rigid or semi-rigid thick throat protector is mandatory.  

**GEN-5.2.1.4**  
The bib of the mask must not fold up.  

**GEN-5.2.1.5**  
When wearing complete head protection (mask + back-of-head protector + throat protector), turning the head in any usual direction must not expose uncovered skin on the head/neck.  

**GEN-5.2.2**  
#### Hand, arm and torso protection  

**GEN-5.2.2.1**  
Only gloves designed specifically for HEMA competition (e.g. Sparring Gloves) or gloves meeting all special requirements may be used. Gloves containing any metal components are forbidden (except minor reinforcement fittings such as eyelets for elastic straps).  

**GEN-5.2.2.2**  
Competition gloves are not required to have a reinforced palm layer, but the full palm must be covered; a simple textile glove layer is sufficient for palm coverage.  

**GEN-5.2.2.3**  
The upper body must be protected by an undamaged fencing jacket or gambeson made of a material that prevents blade penetration and cushions impact or thrust. The jacket is inspected by equipment check staff and the Referee. It must cover the armpit area and have at least 350N puncture resistance. The collar must be designed to catch upward-sliding blades; using folded puncture-resistant material is recommended.  

**GEN-5.2.2.4**  
All competitors must wear rigid chest protection.  

**GEN-5.2.2.5**  
If the jacket alone does not sufficiently protect the elbow and forearm, additional rigid protection is mandatory.  

**GEN-5.2.2.6**  
The whole body surface must be covered by appropriate protective clothing. No bare skin may remain between glove and jacket.  

**GEN-5.2.2.7**  
An 800N (FIE, single-sided) underarm protector is recommended; mandatory introduction is planned.  

**GEN-5.2.3**  
#### Leg protection  

**GEN-5.2.3.1**  
Groin protection is mandatory for all male and female competitors.  

**GEN-5.2.3.2**  
Knees and shins must be protected from front and sides with rigid protection.  

**GEN-5.2.3.3**  
Hips must be protected by the jacket, separate hip protection (e.g. padded skirt) or padded trousers.  

**GEN-5.2.3.4**  
Thighs, groin and hips must be covered by trousers rated at least CE Level 1 (350N) or other approved puncture-resistant material.  

**GEN-5.2.3.5**  
Between jacket and trousers there must be no body surface lacking adequate protection. Waist-high fencing trousers are recommended.  

**GEN-5.2.4**  
#### Weapons  
Each fencer is responsible for their own weapon. The weapon must be presented in proper condition at the competition: free of burrs and rust, and with any previous tip tape removed. Weapons are inspected by designated staff per the requirements below; if compliant, staff will tape the tip with a visible tape. The piste Referee may re-check weapons at any time and may ban weapons with significant damage. After a weapon is banned, the Referee may allow up to 5 minutes for replacement. If the fencer cannot comply within the time limit, they may not continue and may be excluded from the event.  

**GEN-5.2.5**  
#### Longsword  

**GEN-5.2.5.1**  
Only straight-bladed European-type fencing/training swords are permitted. A longsword must have a double-edged blade, a crossguard and a pommel. It must be made of suitable steel (wood, aluminium and plastic swords are forbidden) and must meet the following:  

**GEN-5.2.5.2**  
Severely damaged blades are forbidden; blades may not be bent, broken, burred or rusty. Edges must be blunt and rounded.  

**GEN-5.2.5.3**  
The base of the blade may flare, but all edges and projections must be rounded and blunt.  

**GEN-5.2.5.4**  
The crossguard must be straight with rounded ends. Additional protection (e.g. rings) is not permitted.  

**GEN-5.2.5.5**  
The pommel must have a smooth surface and must not have sharp rivets or spikes.  

**GEN-5.2.5.6**  
Blade flexibility is tested by a bend test against a scale; the maximum kilogram value indicated by the scale represents flexibility. Longsword flexibility must be between 9–16 kg.  

**GEN-5.2.5.7**  
The tip must be at least 1 cm wide.  

**GEN-5.2.5.8**  
The tip must be blunt, rounded, and either flared or bent back in an arc to reduce the risk of piercing.  

**GEN-5.2.5.9**  
The point of balance must not be more than 9 cm beyond the blade-side edge of the crossguard.  

**GEN-5.2.5.10**  
The tip must be taped on-site with a coloured, clearly visible tape for better visibility.  

**GEN-5.2.5.11**  
Overall length must be 120–140 cm.  

**GEN-5.2.5.12**  
Total mass (men’s event): 1300–1800 g.  

**GEN-5.2.5.13**  
Total mass (women’s event): 1100–1600 g.

## Fencing  
<span id="GEN-6"></span>  

### Coming on guard and the position of the fencers  
<span id="GEN-6.1"></span>  

**GEN-6.1.1**  
The fencer whose name is called first must stand on the Referee’s right, except in a bout between a right- and a left-hander where the left-hander is called first.  

**GEN-6.1.2**  
The team with more right-handed fencers stands on the Referee’s right. If equal numbers, the team called first stands on the Referee’s right.  

**GEN-6.1.3**  
The Referee places the competitors facing each other so that their front foot is directly behind the on-guard line.  

**GEN-6.1.4**  
At the beginning of the bout and thereafter, the fencers are always placed in the centre line of the piste.  

**GEN-6.1.5**  
When placed on guard during the bout, the distance between the fencers must be such that, in the point-in-line position with arm extended, the points cannot touch.  

**GEN-6.1.6**  
After a valid hit, the fencers are placed on guard in the middle of the piste.  

**GEN-6.1.7**  
If no hit is awarded, they are replaced where they were when the fight was interrupted. Returning to correct distance must not force a fencer who was inside the rear boundary at the moment of interruption to step back beyond it. If they already had one foot behind the rear line, they remain there.  

**GEN-6.1.8**  
(In direct elimination bouts) at the beginning of each period and any additional 1-minute extension, the fencers are placed on guard in the middle of the piste.  

**GEN-6.1.9**  
The fencers come on guard at the Referee’s command “On guard!”. The Referee then asks “Ready?”; on an affirmative reply or in absence of a negative reply, the Referee starts the bout with “Play!”.  

**GEN-6.1.10**  
Until “Play!”, fencers must remain correctly on guard and completely still.  

**GEN-6.1.11**  
When applying VOR rules/weapon variant, coming on guard may not be done in the point-in-line position.

### Beginning, stopping and restarting the bout  
<span id="GEN-6.2"></span>  

**GEN-6.2.1**  
Before the start of the bout, the Referee asks the opponents to agree whether grappling holds are allowed during body-to-body fighting in their bout. This must be noted on the score sheet.  

**GEN-6.2.2**  
The start of the bout is signalled by “Play!”. Any movement executed or initiated before the command is not counted.  

**GEN-6.2.3**  
The bout stops on the command “Halt!”, except in special situations which modify the regular and normal conditions of the bout (see [GEN-6.10.9], [GEN-6.10.9] as well): [GEN-6.2.4], [GEN-6.2.5], [GEN-6.2.6], [GEN-6.2.7].  

**GEN-6.2.4**  
After “Halt!”, a fencer may not start a new action; only a movement begun before the order remains valid. Everything which occurs afterwards is not valid (but see [GEN-6.10.9], [GEN-6.10.9]).  

**GEN-6.2.5**  
If a fencer stops before “Halt!” and is hit, the hit is valid.  

**GEN-6.2.6**  
“Halt!” must also be called if the fencing becomes dangerous, confused or contrary to the rules; if a fencer becomes disarmed (except in wrestling contexts); or if a fencer leaves the piste/arena.  

**GEN-6.2.7**  
Except in exceptional circumstances, the Referee may not allow a fencer to leave the piste. If a fencer leaves without permission, first-group penalties apply.  

**GEN-6.2.8**  
Fencers may request the bout to be stopped by raising their hand, for any valid reason (e.g. equipment failure, injury). The bout is considered stopped only when the Referee gives the command “Halt!”.  

**GEN-6.2.9**  
Actions initiated before the start command or after the stop command are not valid. Initiating an action before “Play!” or after “Halt!” is a first-group disciplinary offence.  

**GEN-6.2.10**  
The Referee may disregard non-valid hits if they can clearly follow subsequent actions.  

**GEN-6.2.11**  
If the Referee stops the bout because they believed an earlier hit was invalid and ignored it, but it later becomes clear the earlier hit was valid, the Referee must, if possible, decide taking the first valid hit into account, even if this requires annulling later hits.  

**GEN-6.2.12**  
At the end of the pools and every direct elimination bout, before leaving the field of play, fencers check and sign the score sheet kept by the Referee or staff. In team matches this is done by the team captains.  

**GEN-6.2.13**  
In case of equipment failure during the bout, the Referee may allow a break of up to 5 minutes (starting when the Referee confirms the failure). This time may be used for repair or replacement and re-checking. If the competitor cannot continue by the end of the break, the Referee may exclude the competitor from that bout. See [ORG-2.3.13].  

### Close quarters  
<span id="GEN-6.3"></span>  

**GEN-6.3.1**  
Fencing at close quarters is permitted so long as the competitors can wield their weapons correctly and the Referee can follow the action.  

### Body contact  
<span id="GEN-6.4"></span>  

**GEN-6.4.1**  
It is forbidden intentionally to cause body contact to avoid being hit or to push the opponent, except where the fighters have approved the use of gripping and wrestling actions before the bout. In case of such an offence, first-group penalties apply and any hit scored by the fencer at fault is annulled.  

**GEN-6.4.2**  
A fencer does not transgress the basic conventions and is not penalised if, by a flèche attack or vigorous advance, they cause body contact even repeatedly, without brutality or violence.  

### Body contact and flèche  
<span id="GEN-6.5"></span>  

**GEN-6.5.1**  
A flèche systematically ending in body contact (as referenced here) must not be confused with a flèche resulting in a shock that jostles the opponent; the latter is intentional brutality and is punished as a second-group offence at all weapons.  

**GEN-6.5.2**  
Conversely, a running flèche (even passing the opponent) without body contact is not forbidden. The Referee must not call “Halt!” too soon so as not to annul a possible riposte. In a running flèche that does not hit the opponent, if the fencer crosses the boundary, they are considered hit (see: leaving the arena).  

### Displacing the target and passing  
<span id="GEN-6.6"></span>  

**GEN-6.6.1**  
Displacement and ducking are allowed, including where the unarmed hand and/or the knee of the rear leg contacts the floor.  

**GEN-6.6.2**  
Turning one’s back on the opponent during the bout is forbidden. First-group penalties apply and any hit scored by the fencer at fault is annulled.  

### Gripping and wrestling actions  
<span id="GEN-6.7"></span>  

**GEN-6.7.1**  
Gripping and wrestling (grabbing/control/throw/takedown) are permitted within a bout only if both parties consent before the bout starts. If one party does not accept wrestling, no explanation is required. In team matches, each team member decides individually whether they accept wrestling with the given opponent.  

**GEN-6.7.2**  
A wrestling action includes any close-quarter action not executed with the weapon (hand/foot/head techniques, holds, disarms, strikes), as well as weapon actions which, instead of direct hitting, aim to manipulate the opponent’s body parts. If either opponent did not consent to wrestling, initiating any wrestling action is a first-group offence.  

**GEN-6.7.3**  
The Referee may stop the bout after prolonged unsuccessful close quarters or wrestling (at the Referee’s discretion). Grabbing an opponent’s momentarily neutralised blade is permitted. A point may be awarded only if the blade grab is brief and is immediately followed by a weapon hit. If no hit results from the grab, the Referee stops without awarding a point. If the initiating party does not neutralise the opponent’s weapon first and receives a hit, the point is awarded against the initiator.  

**GEN-6.7.4**  
Scoring wrestling actions:  

**GEN-6.7.4.1**  
With a wrestling hold, lifting at least one of the opponent’s feet off the ground. (Throws/takedowns must NOT be completed.)  

**GEN-6.7.4.2**  
Wrestling holds with or without the weapon that prevent the opponent’s movement/escape. The Referee judges whether the control position exists and is valid; once the Referee judges the control/dominance requirements are met, they call “Halt!” and award 1 point against the dominated fencer.  

**GEN-6.7.4.3**  
A dominance/control judgement is not applicable in positions where the opponent can break out and initiate valid actions, even if, by active effort, the dominating weapon continues to touch the opponent’s target. The Referee judges validity at their discretion.  

**GEN-6.7.5**  
After a takedown, further fighting is not permitted. The Referee calls “Halt!” if either fencer goes to the ground, or if the Referee cannot judge the action, or if dominance cannot be obtained over a prolonged period (Referee’s discretion).  

**GEN-6.7.6**  
A disarm (where one fencer, by intentional close-quarter action, takes the opponent’s weapon) is a valid scoring method, except if the disarmed fencer immediately (before “Halt!”) controls the opponent’s blade and continues wrestling.  

**GEN-6.7.7**  
If a fencer drops their weapon or falls without opponent intervention, the Referee calls “Halt!”. A hit achieved by a movement that began before the opponent clearly lost their weapon or fell must be considered valid even if it lands afterwards. By sportsmanship, it is improper to initiate a new attack against an opponent who has lost their weapon or fallen; therefore no point is awarded for such a new attack.  

### Ground gained or lost  
<span id="GEN-6.8"></span>  

**GEN-6.8.1**  
When “Halt!” is called, ground gained is held until a hit is awarded. If no hit is awarded, on re-placing on guard both fencers should retire an equal distance to keep fencing distance.  

**GEN-6.8.2**  
However, if the bout was stopped because of body contact (and close quarters is not allowed), the fencers are placed on guard so that the fencer who sustained the body contact remains where they were; this also applies if the opponent made a flèche attack even without body contact.  

### Crossing the boundaries  
<span id="GEN-6.9"></span>  

**GEN-6.9.1**  
If a competitor crosses completely (with both feet) any boundary line of the arena, the Referee must immediately call “Halt!” and must annul everything that occurred after the boundary was crossed.  

**GEN-6.9.2**  
However, a hit by a fencer who steps out with only one foot remains valid if the action began before “Halt!”, even if the fencer subsequently completely leaves the arena.  

**GEN-6.9.3**  
If a fencer leaves the piste completely (both feet), only a hit made by the fencer who remains at least one foot inside may be awarded, and only in Longsword-Combat and Longsword-Afterblow (even in case of simultaneous hits). In Longsword-VOR, the convention rules apply.  

**GEN-6.9.4**  
If a fencer steps completely beyond a boundary (both feet) without having scored a valid hit before exiting:
- on the first exit, the fencer receives a warning;
- on any further exit in the same bout, the exiting competitor is penalised by a hit against them (the opponent receives one point), even if the competitor was not actually hit.

**GEN-6.9.5**  
**Accidental stepping out:** no penalty applies where a fencer leaves the boundary not intentionally but due to unexpected events/accidents (e.g. being pushed).  

### Duration of the bout  
<span id="GEN-6.10"></span>  

**GEN-6.10.1**  
The duration of a bout means effective fighting time, i.e. the total of time intervals between “Play!” and “Halt!”.  

**GEN-6.10.2**  
Time is controlled by the Referee or a timekeeper. In bouts where the timer is visible to spectators (strongly recommended for finals), the timer must be placed so it is visible to both fencers and the Referee.  

**GEN-6.10.3**  
A finished bout or relay segment may not be restarted, even if there was a formal error.  

#### Framework for individual pool bouts  

**GEN-6.10.4**  
In individual pool bouts, a bout ends when any of the following conditions is met:  

**GEN-6.10.4.1**  
One fencer reaches the defined number of hits (or in Afterblow: the defined score):  

**GEN-6.10.4.1.1**  
**VOR**: one fencer reaches **5 hits**.  

**GEN-6.10.4.1.2**  
**Combat**:
- one fencer reaches **5 hits**, or
- if the score reaches 4–4, a decisive hit must be scored before the time limit ends. Double hits are not taken into account (and the fencers remain where they were on the piste).  

**GEN-6.10.4.1.3**  
**Afterblow**:
- one fencer reaches **at least 7 POINTS**,
	- due to weighting, the score may exceed 7;
	- if both fencers reach or exceed 7 points at the same time (simultaneous hits):
		- if the point difference is at least 1, the bout ends;
		- if the score is tied, the scores must be reset to 6–6 and a decisive hit must be scored before the time limit ends. Equivalent simultaneous hits are not taken into account (and the fencers remain where they were on the piste).  

**GEN-6.10.4.1.4**  
The score sheet records the final score (VN–Dn, where N = winner’s points and n = loser’s points).  

**GEN-6.10.4.5**  
Or three minutes of effective fighting time elapses:  

**GEN-6.10.4.5.1**  
If, when time expires, there is at least a one-hit difference between the fencers’ scores, the fencer with the higher score is declared the winner. The score sheet records the hits/points actually scored in the bout (VN–Dn, where N = winner’s points and n = loser’s points).  

**GEN-6.10.4.5.2**  
If, when time expires, the score is tied, the fencers fence for a decisive hit for up to one minute. **Before restarting fencing**, the Referee draws lots to decide who will be the winner if the score remains tied after the one minute.  

**GEN-6.10.4.5.3**  
In this case, the score sheet records the hits/points actually scored during the bout as follows:
- VN–Dn, if a decisive hit was scored within the time limit;
- V6–D6, or V4–D4, or V2–D2, or V1–D1, or V0–D0, if the winner is determined by drawing lots.  

#### Framework for individual direct elimination bouts  

**GEN-6.10.5**  
An individual direct elimination bout ends when any of the following conditions is met:  

**GEN-6.10.5.1**  
One fencer reaches the defined number of hits (or in Afterblow: the defined score):  

**GEN-6.10.5.1.1**  
**VOR**: one fencer reaches **10 hits**.  

**GEN-6.10.5.1.2**  
**Combat**:
- one fencer reaches **10 hits**;
- if both fencers reach 10 points at the same time (simultaneous hits), they must score a decisive hit before the time limit ends. Equivalent simultaneous hits are not taken into account (and the fencers remain where they were on the piste).  

**GEN-6.10.5.1.3**  
**Afterblow**:
- one fencer reaches **at least 11 POINTS**;
	- due to weighting, the score may exceed 11;
	- if both fencers reach or exceed 11 points at the same time (simultaneous hits):
		- if the point difference is at least 1, the bout ends;
		- if the score is tied, they must score a decisive hit before the time limit ends. Equivalent simultaneous hits are not taken into account (and the fencers remain where they were on the piste).  

**GEN-6.10.5.2**  
Or the defined effective fencing time elapses:
Two three-minute periods (6 minutes of effective fencing time) separated by two one-minute rest breaks.  

**GEN-6.10.5.2.1**  
In **VOR**, the first period lasts either until three minutes elapse or until one of the fencers reaches 5 hits.  

**GEN-6.10.5.2.2**  
If, when the time defined by the rules expires, the scores are tied, the fencers fence for a winning hit for up to one minute. **Before restarting fencing**, the Referee draws lots (coin toss) to decide who will be the winner if the scores remain tied after the additional minute.  

**GEN-6.10.5.3**  
During the one-minute rest break, one person designated before the bout may approach the competitor closely.  

**GEN-6.10.5.4**  
The points recorded on the score sheet are the points actually scored during the bout.  

**GEN-6.10.6**  
#### Team matches  
<!-- TODO Afterblow -->  

**GEN-6.10.6.1**  
Each relay in the team match is fenced to 5 hits (5, 10, 15, 20, etc.). Exceptionally, a relay may end with more than 5, 10, 15, etc. if a fencer scores a valid hit at the end of the relay and an additional penalty hit is also awarded; in this case both are counted. The maximum time for each relay is 2 minutes.  

**GEN-6.10.6.2**  
The first two opponents fence until one reaches 5 hits within the 2-minute time limit. The next two competitors then fence within the 2-minute time limit until one reaches 10 hits, and so on, adding five hits to the target score for each subsequent relay.  

**GEN-6.10.6.3**  
If, after two minutes, the target score for the relay has not been reached, the next two competitors continue the match from the score at which it was stopped, and fence within the 2-minute time limit up to the maximum score prescribed for that relay.  

**GEN-6.10.6.4**  
The winning team is the team that reaches the maximum of 45 hits, or the team that has scored more hits when time expires.  

**GEN-6.10.6.5**  
If, when the time for the last relay expires, the score is tied, the competitors fencing the last relay fence for a decisive hit for up to one minute. Before restarting fencing, the Referee draws lots to decide who will be the winner if the score remains tied after the one minute.  

#### Timekeeping  

Interruption of the fight  

**GEN-6.10.7**   
Each time the fight is interrupted, the fencers may ask how much time remains.  

**GEN-6.10.8**  
A fencer who attempts to interrupt the bout without justification or to prolong interruptions is penalised as a first-group offence.  

**GEN-6.10.9**  
After the regulated time has expired, the timekeeper must call “Halt!” (or operate an audible signal) to stop the fight, and a hit initiated but landing after time has expired is not valid.  

**GEN-6.10.10**  
If no visible timer is provided for the fencers, the timekeeper must give a “One minute!” warning (or audible signal) 60 seconds before the end — without stopping the fight.  

**GEN-6.10.11**  
The Referee remains responsible for timekeeping throughout the bout. In case of timekeeping device failure, the Referee determines the remaining fencing time.  

### Injury or other medical reasons; withdrawal  
<span id="GEN-6.11"></span>  

**GEN-6.11.1**  
If during the bout an injury or other medical issue arises and is certified by the on-duty doctor and/or medical provider, the Referee may allow a break of up to 5 minutes. The break is reserved exclusively for treatment. If the doctor determines the fencer cannot continue, the doctor may decide the fencer must retire (individual) or be replaced if possible (team). All such breaks must be recorded.  

**GEN-6.11.2**  
On the same day, a further break may be granted only for a different injury/medical incident.  

**GEN-6.11.3**  
If a fencer requests a break that the doctor deems unjustified, the Referee penalises the fencer as a second-group offence.  

**GEN-6.11.4**  
In team events, a fencer deemed unable to continue a bout may nevertheless fence in subsequent team matches the same day based on the doctor’s opinion.  

**GEN-6.11.5**  
For efficient running of the competition, the organiser may change bout order in pools.  

### Forbidden actions  
<span id="GEN-6.11"></span>  

**GEN-6.11.1**  
Neck cranks, lifting the opponent, full joint locks, small joint locks, large-arc throws and other potentially dangerous wrestling techniques; strikes (including headbutts, knees, elbows); kicks; violent pushes; and throwing the weapon are strictly forbidden.  

**GEN-6.11.2**  
Excessive force, brutality and unnecessary violence are forbidden.  

**GEN-6.11.3**  
It is forbidden to strike the back of the head, the spine, the back of the knee, and the foot.  

**GEN-6.11.4**  
Hitting the floor due to misjudging distance or movement is a first-group offence (the Referee may ignore light floor contacts that occur after hitting the opponent, as a consequence of the opponent’s action, or accidentally).  

**GEN-6.11.5**  
Hitting with the crossguard is forbidden.  

## Conduct of the bout and judging hits  
<span id="GEN-7"></span>  

### The Referee  
<span id="GEN-7.1"></span>  

**GEN-7.1.1.1**  
By accepting appointment as referee or assistant referee, the person so appointed pledges on their honour to respect the rules and to carry out their duties with strict impartiality and full attention.  

**GEN-7.1.1.2**  
Referees may not simultaneously hold any other role at the competition (e.g. Organising Team member, team captain, official delegate of their federation, coach, etc.).  

**GEN-7.1.2**  
The Referee’s duties include calling and checking in the fencers; directing the bout; checking weapons/clothing/equipment; directing assistants; positioning to follow the bout; penalising offences; awarding hits; maintaining order; and ensuring the arena is safe.  

**GEN-7.1.3**  
After stopping the bout, the Referee consults with the assistant referee if needed, verbally and/or by signals.  

### Assistant referee  
<span id="GEN-7.2"></span>  

**GEN-7.2.1**  
The Referee is assisted by an assistant referee in judging points and evaluating hits.  

**GEN-7.2.2**  
The main task of the assistant referee is to observe the bout from an angle different from the Referee’s (from the other side of the fencers). After the Referee stops the bout, the assistant referee contributes to judging the action if needed.  

**GEN-7.2.3**  
The assistant referee is not responsible for awarding points nor for the final outcome of the bout.  

### Video referees  
<span id="GEN-7.3"></span>  

**GEN-7.3.1**  
When video refereeing is used, designated video consultants watch the bout live and, if necessary, on screen, and consult with the Referee.

### Judging hits  
<span id="GEN-7.5"></span>  

**GEN-7.5.1**  
The materiality of the hit is established by the Referee, consulting the assistant referee if needed.  

**GEN-7.5.2**  
The Referee disregards hits which are made before “Play!” or after “Halt!”, or which are made on objects other than the opponent or equipment.  

**GEN-7.5.3**  
A hit made during or after a fall must be annulled.  

**GEN-7.5.4**  
A competitor who deliberately drops their weapon during the fencing phrase is penalised as a second-group offence. However, a hit scored by the opponent remains valid provided the action began before “Halt!”.  

**GEN-7.5.5**  
In accordance with the general rules, the Referee must stop the bout if the fencing becomes confused and the Referee can no longer follow its course, even if no hit was scored.  

#### Validity or priority of the hit  

**GEN-7.5.6**  
When stopping the fight, the Referee briefly analyses the actions which make up the last fencing phrase.  

**GEN-7.5.4**  
After deciding on the materiality of the hit, the Referee, by applying the rules, determines which fencer a hit must be awarded against (possibly against both), or that no valid hit was scored.  

#### Possibility of video review  
<!-- TODO -->  

**GEN-7.5.6**  
Requesting video review: in both team and individual competitions, only the fencer currently on the piste is entitled to request video review.  

**GEN-7.5.7**  
In individual competition, the fencer on the piste may request video review:
- once per bout in pool bouts;
- twice per bout in direct elimination bouts.  

**GEN-7.5.8**  
If the Referee agrees with the competitor requesting the replay, the competitor’s right to request video review is not lost.  

**GEN-7.5.9**  
In team competitions, competitors have the right to request video review once per relay. If the Referee agrees with the competitor’s request, this right remains available.  

**GEN-7.5.10**  
If video review is requested, the Referee walks to the video referee; together they review the video and, after analysing the action, the Referee communicates the final decision.  

**GEN-7.5.11**  
At the Referee’s discretion, the review is viewed in real time or in slow motion, at a speed chosen by the Referee.  

**GEN-7.5.12**  
At any weapon, the Referee may at any time decide to consult the screen before making a decision.  

**GEN-7.5.13**  
At the end of the bout, if the score is tied, the Referee must use video refereeing before awarding the decisive hit.  

**GEN-7.5.14**  
The video referee may at any time request the Referee to apply video refereeing.  

**GEN-7.5.15**  
Once the Referee has analysed the action together with the video referee — regardless of whether it was initiated by the Referee, requested by the fencer, required due to a tie before the decisive hit, or requested by the video referee — the Referee’s decision is final, and no further review of the same action may be requested.

### Regulation equipment and equipment checks by the Referee  
<span id="GEN-7.6"></span>  

**GEN-7.6.1**  
Before each round the Referee checks the required standard markings and equipment-check markings, and that competitors appear with complete equipment and clothing.  

**GEN-7.6.2**  
Before the start of each pool bout, team match or direct elimination bout, the Referee calls the fencers together to check the following:
- for all weapons, that the fencers’ equipment bears the required 350N marking (clothing, mask);
- that everyone wears a regulation fencing jacket;
- for all weapons, that everyone has a regulation 800N underarm protector worn under the clothing (currently not mandatory, but strongly recommended until a rules change, and must be checked);
- that the weapons bear a weapon control mark;
- for all weapons, that fencers do not carry any electronic communication device that would allow a person outside the piste to communicate with a fencer during the bout.  

**GEN-7.6.3**  
In pool bouts, the Referee’s weapon check is carried out when calling the fencers to assemble.  

**GEN-7.6.4**  
In direct elimination bouts and finals, the Referee’s weapon check is carried out in the assembly area.  

**GEN-7.6.5**  
In addition to the above checks, the Referee leading the bout may at any time carry out checks on their own initiative, or at the request of the fencer or team captain; the Referee may also verify previously performed certification or order additional checks.  

**GEN-7.6.6**  
Checked spare equipment is placed by the Referee at the start of the bout next to the piste, on the appropriate fencer’s side.  

### Non-regulation equipment  
<span id="GEN-7.7"></span>  

**GEN-7.7.1**  
In any situation, if a competitor is found (within the meaning of the Equipment Rules) to possess non-regulation or defective equipment, the Referee confiscates it and submits it to an on-duty expert for examination. The owner may receive the equipment back only after the necessary checks have been completed and any costs arising from repairing the defect have been reimbursed. Before being put back into use, the equipment must be checked again.  

**GEN-7.7.2**  
If a fencer appears on the piste:
- with a defective or non-regulation weapon, or
- in non-regulation clothing,
the Referee applies the penalty corresponding to first-group offences.  

**GEN-7.7.3**  
If during the bout an irregularity in the equipment is identified which may have arisen as a consequence of the fight (for example: a hole in the clothing; rigid protective equipment or glove breaks or falls apart; mask is deformed; the weapon fails), then the Referee applies neither warning nor penalty, and awards any regulation hit scored with the failed equipment.  

**GEN-7.7.4**  
If, when reporting on the piste or during the bout, it is established that the fencer’s equipment:  

**GEN-7.7.4.1**  
lacks a certification/control mark. In this case, the Referee:
- annuls the last hit (if any) scored by the fencer at fault;
- applies the penalty corresponding to second-group offences.  

**GEN-7.7.4.2**  
is defective in a part that was not checked during pre-competition certification. In this case, the Referee applies the penalty corresponding to second-group offences.  

**GEN-7.7.4.3**  
passed pre-competition certification but is suitable for cheating.  

**GEN-7.7.4.4**  
bears a certification mark, but the mark is forged or transferred from elsewhere.  

**GEN-7.7.4.5**  
has been intentionally modified (i.e. not by accident or due to circumstances during the bout) in a way that it would not pass pre-competition certification.  

**GEN-7.7.4.7**  
contains equipment enabling electronic communication, making it possible for a fencer to communicate with a person outside the piste.  

**GEN-7.7.4.8**  
- In the cases [GEN-7.7.4.3], [GEN-7.7.4.4], [GEN-7.7.4.5], and [GEN-7.7.4.7], the Referee immediately confiscates the equipment (weapon and, as needed, jacket, mask, underarm protector, trousers, etc.) and hands it to the on-duty expert.

- After obtaining the expert’s fact-finding opinion, without applying the disadvantages described in [DIS-2.1.4]/[DIS-2.1.6]:
	- in the cases [GEN-7.7.4.3], [GEN-7.7.4.4], [GEN-7.7.4.5], and [GEN-7.7.4.7], the Referee applies the sanction corresponding to fourth-group offences.  

**GEN-7.7.4.7**  
Until the Referee’s decision, the given bout is suspended; however, other bouts of the pool may continue.
