## Longsword / AFTERBLOW  
<span id="LS-AB"></span>  

### Validity and weighting of hits  
<span id="LS-AB-1.1"></span>  

**LS-AB-1.1.1**  
Only the Referee decides on hit validity, applying the following fundamental rules.  

**LS-AB-1.1.1**  
When judging the correctness of hits, the following must be considered:  

- A hit delivered with a correctly held weapon (two hands) is worth 2 points,  
- A hit delivered with a one-handed weapon grip is worth 1 point.  

### Judging hits  
<span id="LS-AB-1.2"></span>  

When applying this longsword rules variant, the Referee must judge as follows:  

**LS-AB-1.2.1**  
When, within one fencing time, both fencers receive a hit in a fencing phrase, this may be either a simultaneous hit or a double hit.  

**LS-AB-1.2.2**  
Hits must be considered simultaneous when the Referee cannot clearly determine the temporal order of arrival of the two hits.  

**LS-AB-1.2.3**  
Hits must be considered double when  

- both hits occur within the same fencing phrase,  
- there is no full fencing-time difference between the two hits, therefore  
- the Referee cannot clearly establish that completion of one hit preceded the start of the movement directly delivering the other hit.  
- The movement directly delivering the hit may be, for example, a simple (direct or indirect) attack/stop-hit/riposte; or, in a compound attack/stop-hit/riposte, the final motion aimed at the final target.  
- Examples (examining action timing, not action priority): stop-hit into a simple attack, or into the final movement of a compound attack, where both actions land.

**LS-AB-1.2.5**  
After-hits  

**LS-AB-1.2.5.1**  
A hit is considered an after-hit and reduces the value (weight) of the original hit if it is executed as follows:  

- **after a fencer has received a valid hit**,  
- the fencer lands a hit with an immediate, **simple**, after-action executed without indecision or delay;  
- without a clearly perceptible break/separation in the fencing, or a re-evaluation of fencing roles.  
- Examples (examining action timing, not action priority):  
    - a stop-hit lands during a feinted attack, **before the final movement of the attack has begun**, but the attacker finishes the attack (the attack lands outside fencing time and therefore qualifies as an after-hit);  
    - an after-hit landed with a **simple riposte after a failed parry (received hit)**  

**LS-AB-1.2.5.2**  
For consistent rule application, the head Referee may determine a clearly perceptible interruption of the fencing phrase based on the following observable events:  

- When the Referee perceives the first hit, the bout is stopped; actions initiated after the halt must be annulled, and these belong to first-group disciplinary offences (cf. General / Conduct of the bout).  
- A clearly perceptible temporal difference exists if, between the two hits, any of the following is observed:  
    - clear withdrawal/raising-back of the weapon, or withdrawal of threat  
    - initiation of the next step instead of/before the after-cut or after-thrust  
    - a moment when the opponent is no longer under immediate threat  

**LS-AB-1.2.6**  
In cases of simultaneous hits AND double hits, both fencers are considered hit. Hits must be awarded as follows:  

- if both fencers landed with a two-handed grip, 1 point must be awarded against both fencers  
- if both fencers landed with a one-handed grip, 1 point must be awarded against both fencers  
- if one fencer landed one-handed and the other two-handed simultaneously, 1 point must be awarded against the fencer who held the weapon one-handed.  

**LS-AB-1.2.7**  
In case of after-hits, the hit must be awarded against the fencer who was hit first in temporal order. The after-hit is not awarded in its own right, but only from the perspective of whether it qualifies as a valid after-hit relative to the first, original hit.

**LS-AB-1.2.9**  
An after-hit (regardless of whether executed with one or two hands on the weapon) reduces the number of points awardable relative to the original hit:  

- if the original hit is worth 2 points, the after-hit reduces the registered score to 1  
- if the original hit is worth 1 point, the after-hit reduces the registered score to 0.  

**LS-AB-1.2.10**  
Defence against after-actions  

<!-- TODO change this to extra point for riposte only when the circumstances are suitable -->  

**LS-AB-1.2.10.1**  
If the fencer who delivered the original hit **defends with the blade against the actually executed after-action** (the fact of the defence is sufficient; a riposte is not required), the registered score is increased:  

- if the original hit is worth 2 points, defending the after-action increases the registered score to 3  
- if the original hit is worth 1 point, defending the after-action increases the registered score to 2  

**LS-AB-1.2.10.2**  
An after-action started before the halt command may be defended, but any further action initiation after “Halt!”, including a riposte from defending the after-action, still belongs to first-group disciplinary offences.  

**LS-AB-1.2.10.3**  
Preventing/deflecting hits within one fencing time (e.g. closing cuts/thrusts) is not considered defence against an after-action.  

**LS-AB-1.2.10.4**  
Defence against an after-action may be awarded only if it is executed after a valid original hit, against an actual valid after-action, with an actual defensive action: i.e. a new, immediate, actual parry movement executed after receiving/landing the original hit (cf. above: After-hits).  

**LS-AB-1.2.10.5**  
The failure or invalidity of an attempted after-action (e.g. too short, misses, falls onto the opponent’s sword) is NOT in itself considered defence against an after-action.  

**LS-AB-1.2.10.6**  
If no actual after-action was executed, defence against an after-action cannot be awarded, not even if  

- the fencer who landed the original hit creates blade contact through attack reprise or blade attack.  
- the opponent, after being hit, initiates an action that does not qualify as an after-action (delay, interrupted fencing phrase, action initiated after halt).  
- Cf. after-hits and their conditions [LS-AB-1.2.5].  

**LS-AB-1.2.11**  
If a simultaneous or double hit is followed by an after-hit within the same fencing phrase, the after-hit must be ignored.  

**LS-AB-1.2.12**  
If there is a double hit or hit-after-hit where one is valid and the other invalid (off-target, delivered after leaving the piste, etc.), **only the valid hit must be awarded**, as if it were the original and only hit. If an additional after-hit then occurs (e.g. a reprise after an off-target hit), it must be ignored.  

**LS-AB-1.2.13**  
If the Referee cannot judge whether an alleged hit occurred or is valid, the hit or double hit is annulled, no point is awarded, and the fencers are reset where the bout was stopped (cf.: 03-general / Ground won or lost).  

**LS-AB-1.2.11**  
If the original hit is invalid (off-target, or delivered after leaving the piste, etc.), the subsequent after-hit is not a scoring action, must be annulled, and the fencers must be reset on guard to the on-guard lines.
