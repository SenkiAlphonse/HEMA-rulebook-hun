## Longsword / AFTERBLOW
<span id="LS-AB"></span>

### Validity and weighting of hits  
<span id="LS-AB-1.1"></span>

**LS-AB-1.1.1**
Only the Referee decides on hit validity, applying the following fundamental rules.  

**LS-AB-1.1.2**
Only intentional hits are considered valid (slipping blades, accidental, or soft hits are not). A valid hit can be achieved with the point, edge, flat, or pommel of the blade.

**LS-AB-1.1.3**
The scoring of target zones is uniform when gripping the sword two-handed grip, and makes no distinction between a cut or a thrust:

| Name of hit | Target zone | Point value |
| :--- | :--- |:--- |
| **Priority** hit | **Mask (Head) and Torso** (including the upper arm) | 2 points |
| **Simple** hit | **Limbs** (legs, and arms from the elbow down)| 1 point |

**LS-AB-1.1.3.1**
The elbow belongs to the forearm, thus cuts landing on the elbow protector are considered Simple, 1-point hits.

(Tip: Strikes landing on the elbow protector have a distinct plastic sound; in case of doubt, this sound or the lack thereof can also help determine the location of the hit.)

**LS-AB-1.1.3.2**
Thrusts arriving from the front above the strap of the elbow protector are considered Priority, 2-point hits.

(Tip: Thrusts arriving from the front usually land on a surface perpendicular to the line of attack, thus they tend to land on the glove, torso, or upper arm. Such hits rarely fall on the forearm. In case of doubt, the Referee should award a Priority hit, assuming it landed on the upper arm.) 

**LS-AB-1.1.4**
A hit delivered with a one-handed grip is always considered a Simple hit and is worth 1 point.

**LS-AB-1.1.5**
A hit to the hilt of the sword is considered a Simple hit and is worth 1 point.

**LS-AB-1.1.6**
A strike with the pommel

- is only valid on the front mesh of the opponent's mask, provided it arrives with controlled force, or the hit is clearly indicated
- is always considered a Priority hit and is worth 2 points
- can also be executed while holding the sword hilt with one hand (for example, while using the non-sword hand to support the other fencer's arm, thereby preventing a simultaneous hit or afterblow)

**LS-AB-1.1.7**
Principle of continuity: If an attack slides continuously to a higher-value zone (e.g., slides from the hand to the torso), the higher point value (2 points) must be considered.

**LS-AB-1.1.8**
If the head Referee judges that a higher-value target zone is intentionally defended with a lower-value one, the Priority (higher point value) zone is awarded. This rule also applies to afterblows.
(Example: After a valid preceding hit, the fencer who scored the preceding hit raises their arms and sword above their head to defend against a potential vertically descending hit, but the afterblow travels horizontally at head height and hits the arm. In this case, the Referee may judge that the fencer who scored the preceding hit intended to invalidate the afterblow by covering the Priority target zone, and may award the afterblow as valid.)

### Judging hits 
<span id="LS-AB-1.2"></span>

When applying this longsword rules variant, the Referee must judge as follows:  

**LS-AB-1.2.1.**  
Preceding hits

**LS-AB-1.2.1.1**  
A hit must be considered an preceding hit when

- only a single hit is scored before the halt command, or
- it is the first in temporal order among multiple hits in the fencing bout, provided that
- the first hit precedes the second hit by at least one fencing time, therefore
- the completion of the first hit preceded the start of the movement directly delivering the second hit

**LS-AB-1.2.1.2**
If a single hit is scored or no valid afterblow occurs (see below), the full point value corresponding to the weighting rules must be awarded.

**LS-AB-1.2.2.** 
Simultaneous hits

**LS-AB-1.2.2.1**  
When, within one fencing time, both fencers receive a hit in the same a fencing phase, this may be either a simultaneous hit or a double hit.  

**LS-AB-1.2.2.2**  
Hits must be considered double when  

- both hits occur within the same fencing phase,  
- there is no full fencing-time difference between the two hits, therefore  
- the Referee cannot clearly establish that completion of one hit preceded the start of the movement directly delivering the other hit.  
- The movement directly delivering the hit may be, for example, a simple (direct or indirect) attack/stop-hit/riposte; or, in a compound attack/stop-hit/riposte, the final motion aimed at the final target.  
- Examples (examining action timing, not action Priority): stop-hit into a simple attack, or into the final movement of a compound attack, where both actions land. 

**LS-AB-1.2.2.3**
In cases of simultaneous hits AND double hits, both fencers are considered hit. Hits must be awarded as follows: 
- the difference in the value of the hits scored by the two fencers must be awarded to the fencer who scored the higher-value hit
- if both fencers scored a 2-point hit, no points are awarded against either fencer
- if both fencers scored a 1-point hit, no points are awarded against either fencer
- if one fencer scored a 1-point hit and the other a 2-point hit simultaneously, 1 point must be awarded against the fencer who scored the 1-point hit.  

**LS-AB-1.2.3**  
Afterblows  

**LS-AB-1.2.3.1**
A hit is considered an afterblow and reduces the value (weight) of the preceding hit if it is executed as follows:  

- **after a fencer has received a valid hit**,  
- the fencer lands a hit with an immediate, **simple**, after-action executed without indecision or delay;  
- without a clearly perceptible break/separation in the fencing, or a re-evaluation of fencing roles.  
- the afterblow is considered a **2-point** hit
- Examples (examining action timing, not action Priority):  
    - a stop-hit lands during a feinted attack, **before the final movement of the attack has begun**, but the attacker finishes the attack (the attack lands outside fencing time and therefore qualifies as an afterblow);  
    - an afterblow landed with a **simple riposte after a failed parry (received hit)** 
	
**LS-AB-1.2.3.2**
For consistent rule application, the head Referee may determine a clearly perceptible interruption of the fencing phase based on the following observable events:  

- When the Referee perceives the first hit, the bout is stopped; actions initiated after the halt must be annulled, and these belong to first-group disciplinary offences (cf. General / Conduct of the bout).  
- A clearly perceptible temporal difference exists if, between the two hits, any of the following is observed:  
    - clear withdrawal/raising-back of the weapon, or withdrawal of threat  
    - initiation of the next step instead of/before the after-cut or after-thrust  
    - a moment when the opponent is no longer under immediate threat  
	
**LS-AB-1.2.3.3**
With an afterblow, a fencer cannot increase their own score; it reduces the score of the fencer who achieved the valid preceding hit as follows:
- If the afterblow qualifies as a Priority hit, a **2-point** hit (Head/Torso): it **deducts 1 point** from the preceding hit's score.
- If the afterblow qualifies as a Simple hit, a **1-point** hit (Limbs): it **deducts 0 points** (does not modify the preceding hit's score).

**LS-AB-1.2.3.4**
The effect of the afterblow on the score:

| preceding hit zone (Points received) | afterblow zone (Deduction) | Final points awarded to preceding hitter |
| :---: | :---: | :---: |
| Simple (1) | Simple (-0) | **1** |
| Simple (1) | Priority (-1) | **0** |
| Priority (2) | Simple (-0) | **2** |
| Priority (2) | Priority (-1) | **1** |

### Effect of penalties
<span id="LS-AB-1.3"></span>

**LS-AB-1.3.1**
If an preceding hit and an afterblow occur in a bout, but the fencer delivering the preceding hit receives a warning (yellow/red card), their hit is considered invalid. In this case, the second hit (the preceding afterblow) becomes the first valid hit, and the fencer scoring it receives the points determined for that zone (1 or 2 points).
