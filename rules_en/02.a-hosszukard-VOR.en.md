## Longsword / VOR (conventional) rules  
<span id="LS-VOR-1"></span>  

### Validity or priority of hits  
<span id="LS-VOR-1.1"></span>  

#### Preliminary notes  

**LS-VOR-1.1.1**  
Only the Referee decides on the validity or priority of a hit, applying the following fundamental rules which constitute the VOR convention for longsword fencing.  

#### Respect of the fencing phrase  

**LS-VOR-1.1.2**  
Every correctly executed attack must be parried or completely avoided, and the fencing phrase must be continuous and coordinated.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.3**  
When judging whether an attack is correctly executed, the following must be considered:  

**LS-VOR-1.1.3.1**  
A simple attack, direct or indirect, is correctly executed when the extension of the arm and the continuous threatening of the valid target with the point precede the initiation of the lunge or the flèche.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.3.2**  
A compound attack is correctly executed when, from the presentation of the first feint with the arm extended, the point threatens the valid target continuously without the attacker withdrawing the arm during the successive movements of the attack and the initiation of the lunge or flèche.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.3.3**  
An attack with a step-forward-lunge or a step-forward-flèche is correctly executed when the extension of the arm precedes the completion of the step forward and the initiation of the lunge or flèche.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.3.4**  
Simple or compound actions, steps or feints executed with the arm bent are not considered attacks but preparations, exposing the fencer to the initiation of an offensive or offensive–defensive action by the opponent.  

#### When analysing the fencing phrase, to judge priority of the attack the following must be considered  

**LS-VOR-1.1.2**  
When analysing the fencing phrase to judge priority of the attack, the following factors must be taken into account:  

**LS-VOR-1.1.2.1**  
If the attack is initiated when the opponent is in the “point in line” position (i.e. with the arm extended and the point continually threatening the valid target — longsword: longpoint/langort), the attacker must first deflect the opponent’s weapon. The Referee must ensure that mere blade contact is not considered sufficient to deflect the opponent’s weapon.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.2.2**  
If the attempt to deflect the opponent’s blade does not find the blade (dérobement / evasion), the right of attack passes to the opponent.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.2.3**  
If the attack is initiated when the opponent is not in point in line, the attack may be executed directly or by disengagement, cut-over, or with feints obliging the opponent to parry.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.2.4**  
Continuous forward stepping with the legs crossing constitutes preparation; against such preparation, any simple attack has priority.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.3**  
In compound attacks, feints must be executed correctly, i.e.:  

**LS-VOR-1.1.3.1**  
A thrust feint must be shown with the arm extended while continuously threatening the valid target with the point;  

**LS-VOR-1.1.3.2**  
A cut feint must be shown with the arm extended such that the blade and the arm form an obtuse angle of about 135°, and the cutting edge threatens the valid target;  

**LS-VOR-1.1.3.3**  
If during the execution of a compound attack the opponent takes the attacker’s blade during a feint, the opponent has the right to riposte.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.3.3**  
In compound attacks the opponent has the right to stop-hit; however, for it to be valid, the stop-hit must precede the conclusion of the attack by one fencing time, i.e. the stop-hit must land before the attacker begins the final movement of their attack.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.4**  
Attack by beat or taking of the blade:  

**LS-VOR-1.1.4.1**  
An attack by beat or taking of the blade is correctly executed and retains its priority when the beat is made on the foible (the weaker half), i.e. the half of the opponent’s blade farther from the guard.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.4.2**  
In an attack by beat or taking of the blade, when the beat is made on the forte (the stronger half), i.e. the half of the opponent’s blade closest to the guard, the attack must be considered incorrectly executed (parried), and this beat gives the opponent the right to an immediate riposte.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.5**  
Parry:  

**LS-VOR-1.1.5.1**  
A parry (or the complete avoidance of the attack) gives the right to riposte. The simple riposte may be direct or indirect; however, to annul any further actions by the attacker it must be executed immediately, without hesitation or delay.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.5.2**  
A parry against thrusts aims to prevent the point from arriving on the target area against which the thrust is directed.  

**LS-VOR-1.1.5.3**  
A parry against cuts aims to prevent the hit from arriving on the target area against which the cut is directed.  

- the parry is correctly executed when it prevents the opponent’s hit from arriving before the offensive action is completed, by closing the line in which the attack would be completed;  
- if a parry is correctly executed, the opponent’s attack must be considered parried even if, due to blade flexibility, the point nevertheless reaches the target.  

### Close quarters  
<span id="LS-VOR-1.2"></span>  

**LS-VOR-1.2.1**  
Priority ends when the strong parts of the weapons meet (including the crossguard), and/or in wrestling actions. If, after contact of the strong parts of the blades, the fencers immediately hit each other, both hits must be annulled. Otherwise, they may separate and continue fencing (under the conventional longsword rules) or transition to wrestling (under the wrestling rules).  

### Judging hits  
<span id="LS-VOR-1.3"></span>  
The Referee should judge as follows when applying these conventions:  

**LS-VOR-1.3.2**  
If within one fencing time only one valid hit occurs, the Referee awards the hit against the fencer who was hit, regardless of its location, force or the manner of execution.  

**LS-VOR-1.3.3**  
When during a fencing phrase both fencers land hits simultaneously, this is either a simultaneous action or a double hit.  

**LS-VOR-1.3.4**  
A simultaneous action results from the simultaneous decision and execution of an attack by both fencers; in this case, the hits exchanged are annulled for both fencers.  

**LS-VOR-1.3.5**  
A double hit, by contrast, results from a faulty action by one of the fencers. Therefore, if there is not one fencing time between the two hits:  

**LS-VOR-1.3.5.1**  
Only the fencer who is attacked is counted as hit:  

- if they make a stop-hit into a simple attack;  
- if instead of parrying they attempt to evade but do not succeed;  
- if after a successful parry they pause momentarily, giving the opponent time to renew the attack (redoublement, remise, reprise);  
- if into a compound attack they stop-hit at an incorrect time;  
- if, having point in line (longpoint/langort), their blade is deflected by a beat or taking of the blade, and they then attack or re-establish the line instead of parrying the direct hit.  

**LS-VOR-1.3.5.2**  
Only the attacker is counted as hit:  

- if they attack an opponent’s point in line (longpoint/langort) without first deflecting the blade;  
- if they search for the blade but do not find it (opponent evades) and they continue their attack;  
- if during a compound attack the opponent takes the blade, the attacker continues, while the opponent ripostes immediately;  
- if during a compound attack the attacker bends the arm or pauses momentarily, the opponent stop-hits while the attacker continues;  
- if during a compound attack the attacker receives a stop-hit in time (one fencing time before the final movement begins);  
- if their original attack is parried and they score by remise/redoublement/reprise while the opponent’s riposte is immediate, simple, in one fencing time, and without withdrawing the arm.  

**LS-VOR-1.3.5.3**  
The Referee must put the fencers back on guard whenever, in case of a double hit, the Referee cannot clearly determine which side committed the fault.  

**LS-VOR-1.3.6**  
A hit that occurs beyond one fencing time (a late after-action) is not relevant to the phrase. Such a hit scores no point and does not annul the original hit received.  

[[FIE_TEXT_NOT_DIRECTLY_APPLICABLE]] These VOR conventions are adapted from FIE foil/sabre “priority” concepts but are applied to longsword; FIE wording cannot be applied verbatim without considering longsword-specific mechanics (e.g. longpoint/langort, strong/weak blade contact, and close-quarter interactions).
